# Decode throughput part of the pie vs vLLM gap on L4 (gpt-oss c8/c16 selects on the run kernel)

gpt-oss-20b's c8 and c16 decodes route 32 and 64 routes over its 32 experts, 1 and 2 an expert, and the L4 profile's `moe.mxfp4_mma_per_from = 4` (copied from the L40S file) kept them off the register-dequant kernels. Being under the 2-an-expert grouping threshold too, they ran the by-route `moe_matmul_select_mxfp4`, which streams an expert's codes once for each route: 0.84 ms a select, about 40 ms of a c16 step whose ITL is 50 ms (nsys). vLLM 0.32.0 runs Marlin at every width. With `= 0`, as on the A40, they take the 512-code run kernel that c25–c128 already decode on, which reads each touched expert once (0.47 ms a select). c17–c24 move to it as well, off the grouped wmma select. `the_routed_selects_answer_the_routed_dot` gains a case at gpt-oss-20b's c16 shape (32 experts, 16 tokens, top 4, by token: 2 routes an expert, on the run kernel on sm_89), where no case covered the run kernel at 2–3 routes an expert.

On an L4, 3 alternating rounds on this tree: c16 301 → 486 tok/s (vLLM 442), c8 243 → 277 (vLLM 276), c24 674 → 665 (−1.3%, where the grouped wmma select beat the run kernel at 3 routes an expert), and c32, whose selects keep their kernel, 836 → 839. Before the rebase, 3 rounds over the site's whole curve (c4–c1024, pf4–pf1024, raw) moved nothing else outside ±1.2% apart from two points the line does not cause: c4 +3.7%, whose selects keep their kernel, and pf8 −2.3%, which is bimodal on this card. The alternative, `moe.mxfp4_grouped_reuse = 0`, gave c8 262 and c16 442, and cost c4 13%. Cross-model: gptoss-20b-mini c8 794 → 888 and a 32-expert MiMo-V2.6-Flash miniature c8 463 → 637, c4 309 → 331, the rest within spread. Greedy tokens are identical for raw and pbatch on all three models. At c8–c24 every decode step changes kernel, and base against fix diverges a little further than one engine's reruns do (mean prefix share 0.36–0.67 against 0.45–0.97), while at c32 it stays inside the reruns' range.
